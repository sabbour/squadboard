# 2. Cast and review the team

Spark needs a small, project-specific team — not a generic pool of assistants. In this tutorial, you will review who is on the roster, fill any role gaps with **Cast Team**, and use **Consult** to think out loud before you commit anything to the board.

This tutorial assumes you completed [Tutorial 1](./connect-spark.mdx) and have Spark open.

## Why this matters

When you move a card to Ready (next tutorial), Squadboard's coordinator decides who picks it up. That decision uses *agent role + charter + skills + labels on the card*, not the agent's name. A roster with thin or missing roles produces bad assignments — so give it five minutes here before queuing work.

## 1. Open the roster

Open **Agents**. Spark from Tutorial 1 should have at least a launch lead and an integration owner already present (either from the template or from your existing `.squad/`).

<Screenshot src="/img/scenarios/spark-06-agents.png" alt="Spark Agents page listing the launch lead and integration engineer with roles and expertise." caption="Each agent needs a name, a role, and expertise. Names alone are not enough for assignment." />

For each agent, confirm:

1. **Name** is stable and easy to reference in cards and labels.
2. **Role** describes an outcome (Launch lead, Integration engineer), not just a technology.
3. **Expertise** covers the recurring decisions Spark will make.

If any agent is missing a role or charter, fix it now. Charters live under `.squad/` and are shared with upstream Squad and Copilot CLI — edits here are durable.

## 2. Fill role gaps with Cast Team

If Spark is missing a role you know it needs — say a reviewer or a docs lead — click **Cast Team**. Cast Team turns "what team should Spark have?" into a reviewable proposal you can edit before accepting.

<Screenshot src="/img/scenarios/spark-07-team-casting.png" alt="Cast Team dialog suggesting a proposed Spark roster." caption="Cast Team is a draft proposal. Accept the parts that fit, edit the charters, and discard the rest." />

Accept only the roles you actually need. A small team with sharp charters routes better than a big team with vague ones.

## 3. Use Consult when the work is still ambiguous

Before queuing a card, you often want to think it through with an agent or with a raw model. That is what **Consult** is for. It is exploration; nothing it produces changes Spark until you create a card or accept a proposal.

Open **Consult → New consult**, pick **Model** (or a specific Spark agent), and ask a narrow question. For example:

```text
Spark needs a launch note that explains Squadboard's relationship to upstream Squad.
What are the three points the launch lead should cover, and what tone should it use?
```

<Screenshot src="/img/scenarios/spark-08-consult.png" alt="Spark Consult page in Model mode with a launch-note prompt drafted." caption="Consult is for brainstorming. The board is for durable execution. Decide which one you need before opening." />

Rules of thumb:

- Use **Consult** when the right answer depends on judgment, tone, or incomplete information.
- Use a **board card** when the answer needs durable execution, audit trail, and a run history.
- When Consult produces something good, promote it: copy the wording into a new board card so the work becomes trackable.

## What you should have now

- A Spark roster where every agent has a name, role, and charter.
- A casting decision recorded (either "current roster is fine" or "added X via Cast Team").
- A Consult conversation that helped you sharpen the next card — or a confirmed plan to skip Consult and go straight to the board.

Continue with [3. Run the Spark launch wave →](./run-the-launch-wave.mdx) to capture work, move it into Ready, watch automatic pickup, and inspect what the agent produced.
